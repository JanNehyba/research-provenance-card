## Agreed changes, not yet made

1. **Plain English.** The current text is dense. The internal reason this
   matters: section 1A(c) argues the standard must not be harder to follow for
   less-resourced language communities, and a densely written submission
   undercuts its own argument. One idea per sentence, no nested clauses.
2. **A paragraph on how this submission was produced**, in the row format the
   submission itself proposes. The submission argues that AI which shapes work
   without leaving a trace in the text is the blind spot of current practice
   (1B.3); not disclosing its own production would demonstrate that blind spot.
3. **A stable identifier for the AI actor** in the disclosure row, explicitly as
   a tool that acted and not as an author. AICID (Vidal & Monperrus, arXiv
   2606.28756) is prior art. **Do not propose an AI ORCID**: ORCID's terms
   define registrants as natural persons, and COPE, a partner in this
   consultation, has published that AI cannot be an author.
4. **A principle against full automation.** The consultation asks for records
   that are "machine-readable and machine-generatable". The more structured the
   record, the more easily an agent produces it, and a fully generated
   disclosure is workslop: complete, polished, and asserting nothing. Proposed
   wording: every record must contain at least one field that cannot be
   completed honestly unless a human did something, and that field must be
   required. In this submission those fields already exist: slot 5 ("what could
   not be checked", required free text) and the named guarantor.
5. **Name the structural versus discursive frame** (Corbin, Dawson & Liu 2025,
   "Talk is cheap"). A standard that prescribes wording is discursive; one that
   defines a record with required fields and a closed verification list is
   structural. **Verify this citation in the original before it is used.**
